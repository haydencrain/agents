# AI Tells

Patterns that make a design or its copy read as "generated by default". Used by the Phase 5b sweep in `design-better`. Every hit gets fixed; none of these are acceptable on the grounds of "it's just a mockup".

The meta-tell for both lists: **it could be any product.** Nothing in the design or the copy could only belong to *this* thing.

---

## Visual tells

### Colour & surface
- Purple→blue or indigo→pink gradients, especially on hero backgrounds and buttons
- Glassmorphism / frosted cards everywhere; `backdrop-blur` on things that don't need it
- Glows, soft drop shadows, and blurred "ambient" blobs floating behind content
- Dark mode with a neon accent as the *default* aesthetic for anything "tech"
- Every card the same rounded-16px white/off-white rectangle with a faint 1px border

### Layout & structure
- The same page skeleton every time: hero + one-liner + two CTAs → logo strip → three-column feature grid → testimonials → pricing → FAQ → footer
- Perfect symmetry; everything centred; nothing bleeds off-edge or overlaps
- Feature grids where every card has identical visual weight (icon, bold title, two-line blurb) so nothing is primary
- Generous-but-uniform spacing — no rhythm, no contrast between dense and airy areas

### Type
- Inter (or a near-identical grotesk) for everything, at the same 3–4 sizes
- Headline patterns: "Build faster. Ship smarter." / "The X for Y" / "Supercharge your…"
- A single word in gradient text inside the hero headline

### Content & detail
- Emoji used as icons; or a generic outline icon set with no personality
- Copy that explains the feature instead of the UI showing it ("Seamlessly integrate with your workflow")
- Fake stats ("10k+ users", "99.9% uptime") and fake partner logos
- Every element labelled — headers over things that are self-evident, tooltips on obvious controls
- Sparkle ✨ icons and the phrase "AI-powered" anywhere

### Motion & polish
- Hover-scale on every card; fade-up-on-scroll on every section
- Three things where one would do — the core failure mode: adding instead of subtracting

## Language tells

### Structure & rhythm
- Triads everywhere: "Fast. Simple. Secure." — three adjectives, three bullets, three benefits
- "It's not X, it's Y" and "Not just a X — a Y" framing
- Parallel constructions stacked until they sound like a chant
- Sentence fragments as headlines. Every. Single. Time.
- Em dashes as the default connector in every other line

### Words that only marketing-bots use
- seamless, effortless, powerful, elevate, unlock, supercharge, streamline, empower, robust, leverage, delve, harness, revolutionize, game-changing, next-level, all-in-one, cutting-edge, intuitive
- "Designed for [modern teams / the way you work / humans]"
- "Meet [Product]." / "Introducing [Product]." / "Say hello to…"
- "Whether you're a X or a Y, [Product] has you covered"
- "Take your X to the next level" / "Your X, reimagined"

### Tone
- Relentlessly upbeat and confident; no dry, plain, or wry register ever appears
- Explains benefits instead of stating facts ("Never miss a deadline again" vs. "Due dates sync to your calendar")
- Over-reassures: "No credit card required. Cancel anytime. Trusted by thousands."
- Tells the reader how to feel: "Beautifully simple", "Delightfully fast"

### UI microcopy
- Buttons: "Get Started", "Learn More", "Try it free" — never a verb specific to the product ("Create invoice", "Add a recipe")
- Empty states that cheerlead: "Nothing here yet! Let's get started 🚀"
- Placeholder people and companies: Jane Doe, John Smith, Acme Corp, "Project Alpha", `user@example.com`
- Generic sample data: Task 1 / Task 2, "Lorem ipsum", "Description goes here"
- Nav always: Features · Pricing · Docs · Blog · About

### What real copy looks like instead
- Specific nouns from the product's domain
- Real-looking numbers with units and precision ("4 invoices due Thursday", not "Stay on top of billing")
- A voice with an edge — one register that is *not* cheerful somewhere on the page
- Buttons that name the action the product actually performs
